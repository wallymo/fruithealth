UI / UX Case Study · Mobile PWA

FruitHealth — a citrus-bright design system for instant produce verdicts.

A shopper points their phone at a fruit. Two seconds later they see BUY, SKIP, or RETURN. The brief was clean, fun, and trustworthy at-a-glance — a long way from the clinical scoring apps that dominate the category.

Snapshot

Three challenges, one design language.

01 · Trust

An AI verdict has to feel honest, not gimmicky. We needed a visual hierarchy that puts the answer first and the disclaimer near it — never hidden.

02 · Speed

Decisions happen at the produce shelf in five seconds. The screen had to be readable from a glance and across glare. No fine print.

03 · Joy

Grocery shopping is mundane. A vibrant, illustrated brand can make a quick utility feel pleasant rather than prescriptive.

The brief

Snap a fruit. Get a verdict.

FruitHealth is a mobile-first PWA. A user takes a photo of a fruit, bunch, or packaged clamshell; an AI service grades ripeness and seasonality and returns one of three actions: buy it, skip it, or return it. Supporting detail — ripeness stage, eat-by window, storage tips, in-season note — sits underneath.

The product team had built the back-end and a working but utilitarian front-end. The brief: dress it in a brand that earns repeat opens.

"We want it to feel like a friendly farmer's-market stall, not a hospital chart."
— Product brief, week 1
Competitive audit

What the category gets right (and where it leaves room).

Four scan-and-score apps shaped the direction. The pattern: each is strong on one of clarity, warmth, or hierarchy — none are strong on all three.

Y

Yuka — food & cosmetics scanner

The category leader for "scan an item, get a score." Excellent at single-glance verdict (a coloured dial 0–100) and an unambiguous green/yellow/orange/red palette.

Took: the discipline of one big colour-coded answer at the top of the screen. The palette as semantic system, not decoration.
Left: the tone is clinical — sharp scoring dial, dense nutrition data, very little personality. Feels like a lab report.
V

Vivino — wine ratings via photo

Polished verdict UX with a star rating front and centre and a community review feed below. Photography-led visual style.

Took: hero photo + verdict + collapsible detail layout. The idea that the photo itself is part of the answer, not a decoration.
Left: premium, desaturated palette. Reads as aspirational adult — not joyful, and a little intimidating to a casual user.
T

Too Good To Go — surplus food marketplace

Vibrant green-and-yellow brand, illustrated style, conversational copy. Excellent personality work.

Took: the warmth. Citrus yellow as a hero colour, illustrative produce iconography, cheerful microcopy.
Left: dense screens with multiple competing CTAs and overlapping cards. The hierarchy gets lost in the playfulness.
F

Fooducate — nutrition grading

A→D letter grade as the headline result, friendly tone, photo-based scanning.

Took: verdict-as-letter — instant, language-neutral, glanceable across a noisy aisle. Confirmation that a single token works better than a sentence.
Left: aging visual style (heavy gradients, cartoonish character). Feels stuck in 2014 and skews young in a way that loses adults.

The gap we're filling

As clear as Yuka, as warm as Too Good To Go, with a verdict-first hierarchy borrowed from Vivino and the language-free clarity of Fooducate. Citrus colours unify the "fresh produce" semantics with the playful tone — every primary in the system is also a fruit.

Moodboard → direction

Reading the board.

The starting moodboard pulled together three things at once: a literal fruit-photography mood, a citrus-pattern illustration mood, and a vintage-ice-cream-shop type mood. Distilled, three signals stood out.

Signal 1

Citrus is the system, not the accent.

Every primary on the board — sun yellow, orange zest, berry pink, leaf green — already maps to a fruit. That's the unlock: the brand palette doubles as the semantic palette. Buy is leaf. Out-of-season is zest. Caution is berry. Cream is the warm ground that lets them sing.

Signal 2

Playful doesn't mean childish.

"Ready Set Eat" lockup is rounded, sticker-like, hand-drawn — but the supporting fruit photography is grown-up and editorial. The system has to ride that line: rounded geometry and warm colour, but type and spacing that read as adult-utility, not toy.

Signal 3

Candara — humanist, friendly, low-contrast.

Candara is Microsoft-only and unsafe on the web, so we map to Quicksand: a humanist sans with the same rounded terminals and friendly geometry. Three weights — Light for hairline supporting copy, Regular for body, Bold for the verdict moments.

Principles

Four words, four rules.

The moodboard's trait words become design rules every screen has to pass.

🍋

Fresh

Lean toward saturated, citrus-bright primaries. Cream over pure white. No grey gradients pretending to be premium.

🌶️

Vibrant

Hero colour blocks earn the screen. The verdict band fills the full width and doesn't apologise — saturation is part of the message.

🥗

Clean

One primary action per screen. Generous spacing — 24px is the small gap, not the big one. Hierarchy beats density every time.

🍓

Playful

Rounded geometry, fruit illustrations as punctuation, conversational microcopy ("Ready to eat", "Take it back"). Never at the cost of clarity.

Method

Atomic Design — five layers, one direction.

The system was built bottom-up using Brad Frost's Atomic Design framework: starting from the smallest tokens and assembling up to full pages. This makes the system inspectable at every layer — designers and engineers can pick the right zoom level for the work in front of them.

Layer 01Atoms
Tokens.

Colors, type styles, spacing scale, radii, shadows, motion. The non-decomposable bits.

Layer 02Molecules

Buttons, chips, inputs, the verdict badge — atoms in the smallest functional combinations.

Layer 03Organisms
Patterns.

Result hero card, history list item, region picker modal — composed of molecules into recognisable UI.

Layer 04Templates
Layouts.

Where organisms live on a page — capture screen, result screen, history screen, error states.

Layer 05Pages
Mockups.

Iconography

Two parallel families. Functional icons (chevrons, camera, history) use a 1.5px stroke, 24px grid for clarity at thumb-tip size. Brand icons (the fruits themselves) use flat-fill illustrations with a 2-tone shadow, big enough to read as decoration.

What's next

Where the system goes from here.

  • Motion tokens. A spring curve for the verdict reveal — colour fills from the photo outward, 320ms. Subtle but characterful.
  • Dark mode. Pulp becomes the ground; cream becomes a chip background. The five citrus primaries hold their meaning at lower luminance — they were chosen for that.
  • Illustrated fruit set. A consistent illustration kit for the top 30 fruits in the knowledge base. Used in history thumbnails and empty states. Single style guide so the team can extend it.
  • Accessibility audit. Verdict colours need a 4.5:1 contrast pair against white text. Berry passes; zest needs a darker pulp text override which is already in the system.
  • Token export. JSON token file (Style Dictionary) so engineers consume the same source as design — no more "is this colour right?" Slack threads.

Reflection

The discipline that paid off most: refusing to add a sixth colour. Five primaries, two neutrals. Every chip, button, illustration, and chart had to compose from those — and that constraint is what makes the system feel like a system, not a colour-picker session. The moodboard already had the answer; the work was trusting it.
